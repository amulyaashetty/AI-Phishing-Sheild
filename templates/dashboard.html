What is Phishing?

Phishing is a cyberattack where attackers pretend to be a trusted organization to trick you into revealing sensitive information or downloading malware.

The term "phishing" comes from "fishing" - attackers cast a wide net hoping to "catch" people who fall for their deception.

Why are Phishing Attacks Dangerous?

  • Identity Theft: Personal information used for fraud
  • Financial Loss: Bank accounts emptied
  • Malware Infection: Malicious software installed on your device
  • Ransomware: Your files encrypted until you pay attackers
  • Corporate Espionage: Company secrets stolen
  • Business Disruption: Entire companies shut down

Types of Phishing Attacks

📧 Email Phishing

Most common type. Fake emails impersonating banks, PayPal, Amazon, etc.

Example:

"Your account has been suspended. Click here to verify your identity."

📱 Smishing

Phishing via SMS text messages.

Example:

"Your package is delayed. Click: bit.ly/xxx to reschedule delivery"

☎️ Vishing

Phishing via voice calls. Attackers call pretending to be from banks or tech support.

Example:

"Hello, this is Amazon Security. Your account detected unusual activity. Press 1 to verify."

🔗 Spear Phishing

Targeted phishing attacks on specific individuals using personal information.

Why Dangerous:

Uses your name, job title, company, and other details to seem legitimate.

🌐 Watering Hole

Attackers compromise legitimate websites to distribute malware.

🎣 CEO Fraud

Attackers impersonate executives requesting urgent wire transfers or sensitive data.

How to Spot Phishing Emails

🚨 Urgent Language

  • "Act now"
  • "Within 24 hours"
  • "Your account will be closed"
  • "Verify immediately"

Why? Urgency makes people act without thinking.

🔐 Requests for Credentials

  • "Confirm your password"
  • "Enter your OTP"
  • "Verify your social security number"

Why? Real companies NEVER ask for passwords via email.

🌐 Suspicious Links

  • Shortened URLs (bit.ly, tinyurl)
  • Links that don't match the domain
  • IP addresses instead of domain names
  • Misspelled domains (amaz0n.com, not amazon.com)

Tip: Hover over links to see the real URL.

📧 Suspicious Sender

  • Generic greeting ("Dear User" not "Dear John")
  • Sender email doesn't match company domain
  • "noreply@" addresses for legitimate companies

✏️ Poor Grammar & Spelling

  • Obvious spelling mistakes
  • Awkward phrasing
  • Mismatched formatting

Note: Modern AI-generated phishing might NOT have grammar errors.

📎 Suspicious Attachments

  • .exe files
  • .zip files with executables
  • Double extensions (.pdf.exe)
  • Unexpected attachments from known contacts

How to Protect Yourself

✓ Verify the Sender

  • Check sender email address carefully
  • Look for slight misspellings (amaz0n vs amazon)
  • Contact the company directly using official phone number or website

✓ Don't Click Suspicious Links

  • Hover over links to see real destination
  • If unsure, visit the website directly by typing the URL
  • Never click shortened URLs in emails

✓ Never Share Credentials via Email

  • Banks and companies NEVER ask for passwords by email
  • Real companies have secure login pages
  • When in doubt, call the official number

✓ Enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

  • Adds extra security layer
  • Even if password is stolen, account is protected
  • Use authenticator apps, not SMS when possible

✓ Use Strong Passwords

  • At least 12 characters
  • Mix of uppercase, lowercase, numbers, symbols
  • Different password for each account
  • Use password manager

✓ Keep Software Updated

  • Enable automatic updates
  • Update browser regularly
  • Keep antivirus software current

✓ Be Skeptical

  • If something feels off, it probably is
  • Take time before clicking or opening files
  • Trust your instincts

If You've Been Compromised

Immediate Actions:

  1. Change Your Password Immediately - Use a different, secure computer
  2. Run Antivirus Scan - Check for malware
  3. Contact Your Bank - Freeze accounts if financial info was compromised
  4. Monitor Your Accounts - Watch for unauthorized transactions
  5. Check Credit Reports - Monitor for identity theft
  6. Report to Authorities - File report with IC3.gov (in USA)
  7. Report to Email Provider - Mark email as phishing/spam
  8. Notify Contacts - If email account compromised, inform your contacts
